Motherhood in Crisis Reflection

Assignment 03 • May 5th, 2026

Motherhood in Crisis by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager and Saidu Bah made me think about how dangerous pregnancy and childbirth can be for women who do not have access to good healthcare. The project focuses on women in Sierra Leone and shows how motherhood can become a crisis when hospitals, doctors, medicine, and transportation are not easy to access. Usually, motherhood is talked about as something happy and beautiful, but this project shows the fear and danger that many women face.

One thing that stood out to me was how the project tells the stories of real women instead of only giving facts and numbers. The women in the story, like Memunatu, Heela, Kadiatu, and Tetteh, each have their own experiences with pregnancy, birth, loss, and survival. This made the issue feel more personal to me. It is easy to read a statistic and forget about it, but it is harder to ignore someone’s personal story. These women are not just numbers, and they are mothers, daughters, wives, and people with families who depend on them.

The interactive format also made the project more impactful. The drawings and storytelling helped me understand the emotions behind the issue. It felt more personal than just reading a normal article. The images showed fear, sadness, and struggle in a way that words alone might not. This reminded me that visual storytelling can be very powerful when it is used to show real problems. It can help people care more about an issue that may not personally impact their own lives.

Before reading this, I knew that healthcare was not equal everywhere, but I did not fully think about how serious that problem is during pregnancy. In some places, people expect to have a hospital nearby or emergency care if something goes wrong. However, in Sierra Leone, getting help can be difficult because of long distances, poverty, and limited medical resources. This made me realize that safe childbirth depends on more than just the mother. It also depends on the healthcare system around her.

Overall, Motherhood in Crisis made me see how important healthcare is for mothers. Pregnancy should not be something that puts a woman’s life at risk just because she is poor or lives far from a hospital. Safe healthcare should be a basic right, not a privilege. The project made me feel uneasy and upset, but it also helped me understand why maternal healthcare is so important. These women’s stories show strength and resilience, but they also show that they deserved better care and support.
